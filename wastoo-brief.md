# WASTOO — COMPLETE WEBSITE DESIGN & IMPLEMENTATION BRIEF

## 1. Project Overview

Design and build the official landing page for **Wastoo**, an Algerian environmental technology startup developing a mobile platform that connects citizens, waste collectors, and recycling or waste-recovery centers.

The website introduces Wastoo, explains the problem it aims to solve, demonstrates the proposed solution, communicates its mission, and announces that the mobile application is coming soon.

This website will be used for:
- Startup competition applications.
- Incubator and accelerator applications.
- Presentations to potential partners and institutions.
- Building public awareness and early community interest.
- Establishing an official online presence for the Wastoo brand.

### Primary objective

Make a first-time visitor understand what Wastoo is, why it matters, who it serves, and what makes its approach useful within approximately 30 seconds.

### Secondary objectives

- Establish credibility and trust.
- Demonstrate that the product is under active development.
- Present a coherent environmental technology brand.
- Encourage visitors to follow the project or register their interest in the launch.
- Provide a professional reference link for application forms and presentations.

### Scope

Build a focused, polished, responsive landing page.

Do not build a dashboard, an administration panel, a complete web application, an e-commerce platform, or a complicated backend.

The mobile application is the main product. The website introduces it.

## 2. Brand Positioning

### Brand name

Wastoo

Always use the official spelling and capitalization: Wastoo.

### Brand personality

Wastoo should feel:

- Modern.
- Friendly.
- Approachable.
- Environmentally conscious.
- Innovative without feeling futuristic or artificial.
- Practical and community-oriented.
- Trustworthy enough for institutions and competition juries.
- Local in relevance, but professional enough for international audiences.

The brand should communicate progress, connection, circularity, and opportunity.

### Brand concept

Wastoo aims to make waste recovery easier to discover and more connected by bringing relevant participants together through a digital platform.

The website should communicate this concept without suggesting that recycling alone solves every environmental problem.

### Tone of voice

Clear, optimistic, confident, and human.

Avoid:
- Corporate jargon.
- Exaggerated startup language.
- Unsupported environmental claims.
- Empty phrases such as "revolutionizing the world."
- Excessive exclamation marks.
- Excessive emojis.
- Fake impact statistics.
- Generic AI-generated marketing copy.

Write concise, meaningful copy that explains actual value.

## 3. Visual Identity

### Official color palette

Use these established Wastoo colors consistently.

Primary emerald:
`#087F5B`

Mint accent:
`#42D6B0`

Dark forest:
`#172B2A`

Recommended supporting colors:

- Secondary background: `#F0F6F2`

- Primary body text: `#263D36`
- Secondary text: `#65756E`
- Subtle borders: `#E0EAE4`
- Dark section background: `#172B2A`

Treat the established three brand colors as the source of truth. Supporting colors must complement them rather than introduce a competing palette.

### Color usage

Use emerald for primary actions, important brand accents, links, and selected interface states.

Use mint for subtle highlights, supporting graphics, decorative details, and selected backgrounds.

Use dark forest for headlines, strong contrast, dark sections, and the footer.

Use warm white and pale green surfaces to create breathing room.

Do not use mint-colored text on white when contrast is insufficient.

Use dark text on mint backgrounds where appropriate.

### Visual style

Use a modern, editorial-inspired environmental technology aesthetic.

Combine:
- Large, well-composed typography.
- Generous whitespace.
- Structured content sections.
- Soft, rounded cards.
- Subtle borders.
- Carefully controlled shadows.
- Small geometric or circular motifs inspired by reuse and interconnected systems.
- Authentic mobile-app screenshots.
- Simple line icons.

The result should look like a thoughtfully art-directed startup website, not a recycled template.

### Avoid visual clichés

Do not use:
- Excessive green gradients.
- Glassmorphism on every component.
- Random floating circles and blobs.
- Excessive glow effects.
- Heavy shadows.
- Oversized emoji illustrations.
- Generic recycling stock photos as the main visual.
- Unnecessary 3D illustrations.
- Constant movement.
- Decorative charts with invented numbers.
- Too many different border radii.
- Large amounts of text centered inside every section.

The visual quality should come from layout, typography, spacing, composition, and real product imagery.

## 5. Typography

Typography must work beautifully in English, French, and Arabic.

### Latin typography

Preferred options:
- Geist.
- Inter.

Use a clean sans-serif family with good readability and a professional modern character.

### Arabic typography

Preferred option:
- IBM Plex Sans Arabic.

Other suitable Arabic fonts may be used if already available and compatible with the project.

Use a consistent typographic hierarchy:
- Large, confident hero heading.
- Clear section headings.
- Comfortable body copy.
- Short supporting labels.
- Readable button text.
- Appropriate Arabic line height.

### Type hierarchy

Desktop hero heading: approximately 60–76 px where the layout allows.

Mobile hero heading: approximately 38–48 px.

Section headings: approximately 32–44 px on desktop and 26–34 px on mobile.

Body copy: approximately 16–19 px.

Supporting labels: approximately 12–14 px.

These are starting points, not rigid requirements. Adjust according to the chosen font, line length, language, and screen width.

Use responsive typography with fluid sizing where appropriate.

Avoid excessively thin fonts and extremely tight line heights.

Arabic text requires appropriate line height, word spacing, and heading widths. Do not force Arabic into Latin typography metrics.

## 17. Multilingual Experience

Support three languages:

- Arabic: `/ar`
- French: `/fr`
- English: `/en`

Use `next-intl` or an equivalent established localization solution if compatible with the project.

### English

Use clear, concise, internationally understandable English suitable for competition juries and partners.

### French

Use natural French adapted to Algerian institutional and startup contexts.

Avoid literal translations that sound unnatural.

### Arabic

Use polished Modern Standard Arabic with natural phrasing.

Make the interface feel designed for Arabic rather than merely translated.

### RTL requirements

When Arabic is active:
- Set the document direction to RTL.
- Set the correct document language.
- Mirror directional layout patterns where appropriate.
- Recheck navigation placement.
- Recheck text alignment.
- Recheck icon direction.
- Recheck card layouts.
- Recheck form controls.
- Recheck buttons and dropdown menus.
- Recheck mobile navigation.
- Recheck punctuation and mixed Latin/Arabic text.
- Ensure that decorative alignment does not conflict with reading direction.

Do not blindly mirror every icon. Icons representing physical objects or universal concepts may retain their original orientation.

Switching languages should preserve the corresponding page and work consistently.

Translate the entire website, not just the hero.

Localize metadata, form errors, accessibility labels, FAQ content, and footer text.

## 18. Motion and Microinteractions

Motion should support usability and visual polish.

Allowed:
- Subtle button hover transitions.
- Small changes in card elevation.
- Gentle section entrance transitions.
- Subtle screenshot reveal effects.
- Smooth scrolling when appropriate.
- Lightweight navigation transitions.

Requirements:
- Prefer CSS transitions for simple effects.
- Use Motion only when a more complex interaction genuinely benefits from it.
- Respect `prefers-reduced-motion`.
- Avoid blocking the first render with animation.
- Avoid large parallax effects.
- Avoid continuous floating animations.
- Avoid scroll-jacking.
- Avoid animations that delay access to content.
- Ensure that motion works consistently in RTL and LTR layouts.

The website must remain visually polished when all nonessential animations are disabled.

- Small mobile: approximately 320–375 px.
- Standard mobile: approximately 390–430 px.
- Tablet: approximately 768 px.
- Laptop: approximately 1024–1440 px.
- Wide desktop: approximately 1600 px and above.

Requirements:
- No horizontal overflow.
- No clipped Arabic headings.
- No excessively long lines of text.
- No tiny product screenshots.
- No overlapping hero elements.
- No inaccessible mobile menu.
- No oversized sticky navigation.
- Comfortable tap targets.
- Appropriate image cropping.
- Consistent spacing.
- Proper section transitions.

Avoid simply stacking every desktop element without rethinking its mobile composition.

Preferred stack:
- Next.js App Router.
- TypeScript.
- Tailwind CSS v4.
- `next-intl` for localization.
- Lucide React for icons.
- shadcn/ui selectively where useful.
- Vercel for deployment.

Inspect the repository before implementing the design.

Reuse existing dependencies and conventions where possible.

Keep the website mostly static.

Do not create a separate Express server or database unless a real feature requires it.

Do not duplicate the mobile application's backend.

Keep content, translations, and reusable UI components organized.

Use server components by default and client components only when interaction requires them.

## 23. Design Quality Standards

The final result must not look like a default SaaS starter template.

Before completing the work, review the entire page as a visual designer.

Ask:

1. Is the product understandable within the first screen?
2. Does the hero have a strong visual hierarchy?
3. Does the site feel specifically designed for Wastoo?
4. Are the brand colors used consistently?
5. Do the mobile screenshots feel authentic and prominent?
6. Does the Arabic version look intentionally designed?
7. Is every section useful?
8. Are the calls to action clear?
9. Does the page feel credible to a competition jury?
10. Does the mobile version feel as carefully designed as the desktop version?
11. Are the images, fonts, and spacing consistent?
12. Does the page remain attractive without animation?
13. Is the copy specific rather than generic?
14. Are there any invented claims or unfinished placeholder elements?

Revise any weak sections before delivery.
